Ungated checklist

In-house hire versus a studio

Write the full year, not the salary on the job post. Then set that number next to a studio fee that arrives with a team.

The lines a salary hides

  1. Employer taxes, benefits, and insurance.
  2. Cameras, light, audio, computers, licenses, repairs, and drives.
  3. Ramp-up. Three to six months is common before a full solo slate, and overlap with freelancers during that time is a cost.
  4. The jobs inside the role: shoot, edit, grade, sound, and delivery are not one task.

Match the model to the volume

  1. Daily internal capture, a private set, and a full calendar can justify a hire.
  2. Spiky campaigns, live events, and overflow still need a studio even after the hire.
  3. A hybrid is common: an internal producer who knows the brand, and a retainer for the craft that should not be learned on a client's dime.

Questions to take into the meeting

  1. What does the retainer include, by name?
  2. Who grades, and is that a person or a leftover Friday?
  3. What happens in the week the calendar doubles?

These are studio field notes, not tax, legal, or financial advice. Run the numbers with your own accountant before you hire.
