How DJs Build a Career, Not Just a Set

By Cody Cochran · September 25, 2026 · 5 min read

A great set gets a DJ through one night. A career is the next forty nights, the promoter who can find the work without a text thread, and a record of what the night actually cost. The mix is the craft. The career is the system around it. That system is what DJs Need Luv 2 is built to be: a homebase for working DJs, in the same family of platforms as Creator Collective.

The booth is not the business

Most DJs can tell you the room, the time, and the records. Fewer can tell you the mileage, the deposit, or the page a stranger would land on at midnight. The gap is why good selectors stay busy and broke. School of Hard Knocks has spent years interviewing operators who learned this in public. James Dumoulin's point, repeated across those conversations, is that the visible skill is only one engine. The business needs more than one way to get paid, and it needs the unglamorous records that survive a slow month. The channel is @theschoolofhardknocks.

DJs do not need a lecture about taste. They need a place where the career can be seen and counted. DJs Need Luv 2 is that place.

What the platform actually gives you

The product is a member home, not a flyer template. Here is what is in the build, because this is the difference between a slogan and a tool.

  • A public profile and portfolio studio. Work, music, about, and a page you can send. The studio includes search title, description, and a share image. On a custom domain you own, you can connect Google Search Console. The same studio prepares the page for AI crawlers, including llms.txt, so the portfolio can be understood by answer engines and not only by a person who already has your Instagram.
  • A gig tracker. Log the night, the expenses, the mileage, and the receipts. "Wedding at The Fox" becomes a line the books can explain. That is how a Saturday stops disappearing into cash in a pocket.
  • A music crate. The work can be heard inside the platform, not only promised in a caption.
  • A community board. Posts, conversation, and a room of people who also work nights. Referrals live here more honestly than they live in a comment section.
  • Ranking. Showing up has a score. A leaderboard makes the habit visible.

None of that replaces practice. It makes practice legible to the person who books you.

How a DJ grows from here

Send one URL. The portfolio should answer genre, city, proof, and how to ask for a date. After the gig, log it before you sleep. Expense and mileage are part of the rate, the same way a videographer's drive and tax are part of the rate in the profit essay. Follow up while the promoter still remembers the floor. Ask for the next date, a photo they can post, and an introduction. Volume of clean nights beats one heroic set nobody can find in the morning. That is the same lesson as volume beats waiting.

If you also shoot the night, or you hand a recap to the venue, the files need a real home. Bizzi Cloud is storage, review, galleries, and branded delivery for that media. A dying transfer link is a bad look on a night that otherwise went well. Plans start at $22 a month for 500 GB, with Gallery Suite included on paid plans.

The next booking goes to the DJ a stranger can understand at midnight, not to the DJ with the best unlisted folder.

The wider room

DJs who also want paid creative work, pro assets, invoicing, and a national creator community should be in Creator Collective too. It is the membership Cody Cochran and JB run for videographers, photographers, and editors, and it is the right adjacent room when the career expands past the booth. Watch them at Creator Collective on YouTube. Use DJs Need Luv 2 as the DJ home. Use Creator Collective when the business needs gigs, training, and a payout path. Use Bizzi Cloud when there are files a client must receive intact.

Practical takeaways

  • Publish one portfolio URL and put it everywhere you already talk to promoters.
  • Log every gig, with expenses and mileage, in the tracker.
  • Let people hear the work in the crate, not only see a flyer.
  • Ask for the next date before you leave the building.
  • If you deliver video or photo from the night, send it through Bizzi Cloud.

A direct next step

Open djsneedluv2.com, build the public page until a stranger could book you from it, and log the last three gigs with what they cost you. The mix got you respected. The system gets you rehired.
