Keep More of Every Dollar

By Cody Cochran · September 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Getting paid is not the same as keeping the money. A job can look like a win at the top and arrive thin after the platform, the processor, the tax, and the gear. Creators who only negotiate the day rate, then hand the payout to a stack of fees, work harder for a smaller business. The move is to keep more of every dollar you already earned.

Read the fee before you celebrate the gross

On Creator Collective, Collective Member is $37 a month. The platform fee on marketplace and job payouts is 4 percent. Stripe still takes about 2.9 percent plus 30 cents. The fee applies to the total job amount, not only the deposit. Collective Pro is $60 a month. The fair-use line is 0 percent on the first $200 of eligible payouts in a month, then 2 percent. Stripe still applies. Pro also includes client invoicing, unlimited daily asset downloads, strategy sessions, and live editing sessions.

Member downloads are capped at 10 files or 2 packs a day. Pro removes that cap. If assets are part of how you deliver, the cap is a business decision, not a perk. Run the last three payouts through both fee lines. If Pro costs $23 more than Member and saves more than $23 in fees and time, the upgrade already paid for itself. The site is creatorcollective.media. The longer split of a $10,000 invoice is what you actually kept.

Invoicing is how you stop chasing

A payout fee is only half the leak. The other half is the invoice you send late, the deposit you never asked for, and the client who "will send it next week" while you edit. Collective Pro puts client invoicing in the membership. Jobs on the platform can use deposits and a clearer dispute path, which is how you avoid shipping finals into a void. None of this is a guarantee you will be booked. It is a way to keep the booking you already won. Keeping clients is its own discipline, covered in stop chasing new clients.

Pro members can also run a paid community of their own, priced between $10 and $200 a month. Creator Collective takes 20 percent, capped at $4, and Stripe is separate. That is a second product beside client work, for creators who already have a room that wants to pay them. It only stays up while Pro stays active.

Subscriptions are a fee too

Bizzi Cloud's public site argues that collapsing storage, review, and delivery into one plan is worth about 35 percent against paying those tools separately. Check it against your card statement. Plans start at $22 a month for 500 GB, $30 for 2 TB, $55 for 5 TB, and $100 for 10 TB, with Gallery Suite included. If you are paying for a drive, a proofing seat, and a gallery product, Bizzi Cloud is the line item to consolidate. Money kept is money you do not send to a tool you have outgrown. The product breakdown is in one cloud for the whole job.

The expensive habit is not the membership. The expensive habit is letting every middleman take a cut of work you already did.

Practical takeaways

  • Put the last three payouts on paper with platform fee, processor fee, and what remained.
  • Compare Member at 4 percent with Pro's fair-use line before you assume the cheaper plan is cheaper.
  • Invoice when the work is approved. Ask for the deposit before the date is yours.
  • Cancel tool subscriptions that duplicate Bizzi Cloud once the files have moved.
  • Split what you keep into tax, pay, and reinvestment the day it lands.

A direct next step

Watch the episode, open the membership comparison, and run one real payout through both fee lines. If Pro wins, switch. If your storage stack is the larger leak, move that job to Bizzi Cloud the same week. Keeping more is a decision you can make on numbers you already have.
