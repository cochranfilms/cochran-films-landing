One Cloud for the Whole Creative Job

By Cody Cochran · September 27, 2026 · 5 min read

A finished film still fails if the handoff is a pile of logins. One drive holds the rushes. Another app holds the comments. A third product holds the gallery. A fourth link expires on Friday. The client experiences four brands and one confused thread. Bizzi Cloud was built to end that split. It is cloud storage, client review, media galleries, collaboration, and delivery in one workspace, at bizzicloud.io.

James Williams, the founder, put the reason in public: photographers, filmmakers, and agencies were paying for disconnected tools to manage one project. The fix is one connected place for storage, collaboration, review, and delivery. Cochran Films already treats that handoff as part of the film. The essay on moving client footage securely is the studio version of the same rule.

What you are actually paying for today

Count the subscriptions before you count the terabytes. A storage plan, a review seat, a gallery product, and a transfer tool can each look cheap. Together they cost more than the work, and they cost context. Versions get named final, final-2, and final-client. Comments live in email. The gallery password lives in a text from March. Every extra login is a place for the job to stall.

Bizzi Cloud publishes the consolidation as a real savings, about 35 percent against a stack like Dropbox plus a review tool plus a delivery product. Treat that number as their claim, then check it against your own invoices. If three bills are doing one job, the math is usually obvious.

What a paid plan includes

Every paid plan includes Bizzi Gallery Suite, file sharing and delivery, advanced organization, transfer-link invoicing, and unlimited branded transfers. Subfolders exist because photo and video libraries are not a single dump. Dashboard colors can match the brand the client already hired. AES-256 encryption covers files at rest and in transit on every plan. There is a desktop app for people who live in a folder all day.

  • Bizzi Creator. $22 a month. 500 GB. One seat. Built for a solo creative.
  • Bizzi Pro. $30 a month. 2 TB. Up to 3 seats at $10 each. The plan most teams start on.
  • Bizzi Network. $55 a month. 5 TB. Up to 8 seats. Built for a studio that collaborates every week.
  • We Bizzi. $100 a month. 10 TB. Up to 10 seats, a dedicated account manager, and priority onboarding.
  • Raw Unlimited. $50 a month for photographers who need unlimited RAW and JPEG storage, galleries, proofing, and branding. Added seats are $20, up to 4.

Annual billing saves 18 percent on the base plan and on team seats. Power-up add-ons stay monthly. Enterprise starts around 15 TB with custom seats and a 99.99 percent uptime SLA. Seats can be added after signup, which matters more than it sounds. You should not hire a payroll to share a folder. The companion piece on keeping a bench instead of a salary is the people side of that seat.

How a job should move

Upload the active work and put it in a project with nested folders a stranger could understand. Invite the client or the editor to review, select, and approve in that same place. Deliver with a branded transfer or a gallery when the cut is actually done. If the month gets heavier, add Gallery Suite depth you already have, or Editor and Full Frame when the workflow needs more horsepower. The path is short on purpose. A client should never have to ask which link is the real one.

The file is not delivered when it leaves your laptop. It is delivered when the client can find it, trust it, and pay from the same place.

Where Creator Collective fits

Bizzi Cloud holds the work. Creator Collective is the membership that helps you book the work, price it, and keep more of the payout. Membership starts at $37 a month. Collective Pro is $60 and adds client invoicing, lower fees on eligible payouts, and the option to run your own paid community. The YouTube channel, Creator Collective, is where Cody Cochran and JB keep the operating conversation in public. Storage without a business is a hard drive with a logo. A community without a delivery path is a group chat. Use both.

Questions worth answering before you switch

What is Bizzi Cloud?

It is creator cloud storage with review, galleries, folders, branded transfers, and team seats on one subscription. Gallery Suite comes with paid plans.

How much does it cost?

Monthly prices on the public page run $22, $30, $55, and $100 for 500 GB, 2 TB, 5 TB, and 10 TB. Seats are $10 a month on those team plans. Photographers can look at Raw Unlimited at $50.

Who is it for?

Solo creators, photo and video teams, and studios that are tired of explaining a folder structure that lives in three products. If the client work is large, nested, and needs a clean handoff, it is for you.

Practical takeaways

  • List every storage, review, gallery, and transfer bill you paid last quarter.
  • Put the next project in one workspace before you add another login.
  • Name folders for the client, not for your memory.
  • Send one branded link when the job is finished, and invoice from that same flow when the plan supports it.
  • Add a seat when a collaborator needs access. Do not share the owner login.

A direct next step

Open your last delivery and count the tools it touched. Then open Bizzi Cloud and match a plan to the terabytes you already have, not the terabytes you hope to grow into. If the harder problem is pricing and clients, start with Creator Collective the same day. The files and the business should finally live next to each other.
