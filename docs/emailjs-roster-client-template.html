From the careers guide.

The reel we would book

Three pieces. Each one under a minute. Each one a job a stranger could hire: a church, a room, a dish, a listing, or a shop. Leave a start, a middle, and a finish. Cut the gear shots and the montage of everything you have ever filmed.

The path that gets you booked

Pitch, quote, contract, deposit, shoot, deliver, review, rebook. Skip a step and the next client feels it. Price the finished job, not only the hours on set. Add your monthly business expenses to the income you need, set aside tax and savings, then divide by the days you can truly bill. That number is a floor, not a market price. Revenue is not profit.

Where first clients already are

Churches, venues, restaurants, real estate, nonprofits, and barbershops. One offer for one kind of room. Say what done looks like.

The kit, in three stages

Starter is the camera you already trust, one lens, a mic, and one light you can set alone. Working is a cinema camera, a real light, audio, and batteries for a full day. Studio adds a second body, and air when the job needs it. Buy the tool that is failing a real job. Ignore the rest until then.
